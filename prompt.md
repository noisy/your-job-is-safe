# Prompt: music video for "Your Job Is Safe!"

Build a music video for the song in this folder, with every pixel generated by code.

## Materials (read both fully before anything else)

- `Your job is safe.mp3`: the final song (3:24). This is the soundtrack. Don't compose or change the music.
- `handoff.md`: the story, the full lyrics, the irony and a case-by-case explanation of every reference.
  Rule from it: the lyrics only wink at each AI fail; **the video is what shows the viewer what actually happened**
  (and, later, that each fail got fixed).

## Hard constraints

- **100% generated in code.** Draw every frame in a web page: three.js/WebGL, Canvas 2D, shaders, SVG. No video editor,
  no AI-generated images or video, no stock footage, no photos. Don't use the Gemini cover art in the video either.
- Render frame by frame in headless Chrome via Playwright. Every frame must be a deterministic function of song time:
  seeded randomness only, never `Math.random()` or wall-clock time. Mux with FFmpeg into a **1920×1080, 60 fps H.264 MP4**
  with the mp3 as the audio track.
- **Frame 0 is a designed title card**, not black. Social platforms use it as the preview.
- Real people and brands: no photorealistic likenesses (Will Smith, Geoffrey Hinton, …) and no imitation of real logos
  or product UIs (ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok, Tesla, Waymo, McDonald's). Refer to them with stylised, clearly original drawings
  and plain text labels.
- Don't ask clarifying questions. Make the creative decisions yourself. There is exactly one planned checkpoint (the
  end of Phase 1).

## Reference and quality bar

Study **pdoom-video** (https://github.com/mexicat/pdoom-video, video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5EoO5413dBY), then
**clone it and render its frames yourself** (contact sheets across the whole song, and around its cuts) to understand the
technique. Don't just read the README. That is the level of detail, pacing and lyric integration expected here.

- You may fork its engine (MIT: keep its license and credit Giacomo Magnanini / mexicat). It gives you timeline,
  post-processing, motion blur, typography and GPU lines.
- Don't copy its look or its motifs: our video needs its own visual identity.

## Pacing and craft: the part that is easy to get wrong

A first attempt that "shows lyrics over some animated scenes" is not acceptable. Specifically:

- **Nothing sits still.** No composition holds longer than about one bar (~2 s) without a sub-cut, a reframing or a strong
  camera move. A title or a key object is on screen for seconds, never ten. Big changes land on the beat: cuts on
  downbeats, hits on kicks and snares, camera moves that ease into the downbeat. Use strong eases (anticipation → snap →
  settle), never floaty screensaver drift.
- **3D first.** Every scene lives in a 3D world with a moving camera: dolly, crane, orbit, whip-pan, crash-zoom, roll.
  2D material (type, UI, charts, drawings) goes on planes *inside* that world. It is tilted, parallaxed, flown past and
  flown through. It must never be a flat static card.
- **Scenes morph into each other.** The last frame of a scene visually continues into the first frame of the next,
  through a shared object, shape, line or light. Hard cuts are allowed only on downbeats.
- **Lyrics are on screen for the whole song**, synced **word by word** to the vocal:
  - Appear/highlight at the word's start and complete by its end. Up to 0.4 s of anticipation is allowed, running ahead
    of the voice is not.
  - They are **integrated into the image with intention**: typed into a terminal, written on the glass of wine, part of
    the clock face, stamped, carved, built from objects, riding a path, part of the robot's display. They are not
    subtitles on top.
  - **Every scene uses a different timing idiom:** per-character typing, pen-written, one word per hit, built/assembled,
    riding a curve, a glyph wipe, words as physical parts of the scene, held notes stretching the glyphs.
  - Every line must be readable at 1080p.
- **One consistent style** across the whole video: one palette, one type system, one grain/finish, one camera language.
  Scenes vary their idiom, not their identity.
- **Tell the story visually.** Follow `handoff.md`:
  - show each real AI fail concretely, the way it actually happened;
  - build the dramatic irony (the viewer sees what the narrator refuses to see);
  - make the final chorus visibly mirror the first one, with the same images turned against him;
  - land the doorbell / robot-plumber ending exactly on the timestamps given there.

## Timing data

Make the analysis the video is synced to, and save it as data files the renderer reads. Never hard-code times inside
scenes.

- Separate the vocal (Demucs).
- Force-align the known lyrics from `handoff.md` to it for **word-level** timings: CTC / wav2vec2 alignment,
  cross-checked with Whisper word timestamps.
- Detect beats, downbeats and bars, kick/snare/hat onsets, sections, and loudness envelopes (per stem and per band).
- The spoken intro has no music. Align it to the voice too.

## Phase 1: style exploration, then STOP

Before building the video, propose **10 radically different visual styles or techniques**. Examples of the range meant
here:

- engraved line art;
- raymarched SDF 3D;
- voxel / low-poly;
- risograph halftone;
- blueprint / CAD;
- terminal / ASCII-on-3D;
- claymation-like soft shaders;
- neon-free synth-free vector;
- paper cut-out in 3D space;
- CRT / oscilloscope.

Pick your own; they must be genuinely different, and none may be generic "AI aesthetic" (no purple/cyan neon, glowing
brains, Matrix rain, synthwave sunsets, lens-flare soup).

- Build a **Storybook** (e.g. `@storybook/html-vite` or `@storybook/react-vite`) with one story per style. Each story is
  **a real scene from this song**, and all 10 render **the same moment** so they can be compared directly:
  - use the first chorus, *"It can't count the R's in strawberry / It can't fill a glass of wine"*, about 8–10 s;
  - include its word-synced lyrics and beat-synced motion;
  - add a play/scrub control with the audio of that segment.
- For every style, add a short note: technique, palette, typography, how lyrics are integrated, estimated render cost,
  risks.
- Also render **one still and one 5-second MP4 clip per style** into `out/styles/`, plus a single contact sheet comparing
  all 10.
- Then **stop and report**: the Storybook URL/command, the contact sheet, the 10 notes and your recommendation. Wait
  for me to choose. I may pick one, or combine two.

## Phase 2: the full video (after I pick a style)

1. Write a treatment / style bible and a scene-by-scene plan, anchored to the aligned lyrics and the beat grid. Include
   the lyric idiom per scene and the morph into the next scene.
2. Build the scenes. If you use sub-agents, give each the same style bible and let each own separate files.
3. Check everything yourself before rendering:
   - contact sheets of the whole song;
   - frames on both sides of every cut, where the handoffs must match;
   - lyric sync spot-checks against the vocal;
   - render cost (keep frames fast enough to render the whole song in about an hour).
4. Render the final **1080p60 MP4 with motion blur**, and report what's in it, section by section, with timestamps.

## Deliverables

- `video/`: the project (renderer, scenes, data, analysis scripts, docs), runnable from a README.
- `out/your-job-is-safe.mp4`: the final render (1080p60), plus a web copy at about 16 Mbit/s.
- `out/styles/`: the Phase 1 stills, clips and contact sheet.
